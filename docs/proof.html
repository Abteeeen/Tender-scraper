A

ITO — Security Service · Standing Offer Arrangement (SOA)

Security · Manpower/Guarding Statewide QLD Closes 14 Jul, 2:00 PM SOA · 7 yr + 3×1 yr
Compliance requirements

Company must hold a Security Provider Licence under the Security Providers Act 1993 (Qld), and any applicable Labour Hire Licence. Suppliers must be members of an approved industry association (e.g. BSCAA, SPAAL, ASIAL). Security Officers must hold a valid Queensland Security Licence and, where applicable, a Blue Card. Public and product liability insurance to a minimum value of $20 million per occurrence. Workers compensation insurance as required by law. Adherence to the Queensland Government Supplier Code of Conduct and Information Privacy Act 2009 (Qld), Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) and relevant WHS and anti-discrimination legislation.

Pricing structure

Pricing is to be provided in Australian Dollars, inclusive of GST. The Price for Security Services comprises the applicable Security Services Industry Award (SSIA) base hourly rate — including mandatory penalty rates, overtime and loadings — plus a fixed administration fee. The administration fee is fixed for the Term and must not vary by reference to penalty rates, overtime, shift loadings or public holidays. Annual SSIA adjustments from the Fair Work Commission Annual Wage Review apply to the base rate only. Fuel, mileage and vehicle costs are deemed included unless stated otherwise. Invoices submitted monthly within ten (10) Business Days of the end of the billing period.

Scope of work

The Standing Offer Arrangement (SOA) is for the provision of static guarding, mobile patrols, alarm response, foot patrols, guard-with-canine patrols and other security services to Queensland Government facilities and assets state-wide. Services can be scheduled or ad-hoc, on a per-minute, hourly, weekly or 24/7 basis including public holidays. The primary objective is to secure government facilities and assets, ensuring safe and effective operational environments. Deliverables include appropriately licensed and trained security officers, detailed incident reporting, and adherence to strict performance metrics and governance requirements.

KPIs & SLAs

KPIs include: Site Attendance (100% attendance for all scheduled shifts, 0 unattended sites or unapproved early departures); Invoicing (100% submitted within 10 Business Days, correct on first submission); Major Incident Reporting (verbal within 1 hour, written within 24 hours); Standard Incident Reporting (within 24 hours); Security & Compliance (deviations reported within 1 hour). Failure to meet KPIs results in warnings, service credits and potential termination for repeated breaches. Reporting includes daily attendance, monthly operational and ad-hoc reports on request.

Submission & evaluation

Submission: Offers lodged electronically via the VendorPanel website by 2:00 PM AEST, Tuesday 14 July 2026, in Microsoft Word and Adobe PDF formats. Part C: ITO Response Schedules (Schedule A, Response Book 1 – Requirements, Response Book 2 – Pricing, Schedule B for alternatives) plus Supplier Acknowledgements and Certifications must be completed. Evaluation: Mandatory criteria (pass/fail) — Supplier Code of Conduct, Security Firm Licence, Labour Hire Licence, approved industry-association membership. Desirable — Capability, Capacity, Process, Personnel Management, and Cost/value for money.

Red flags

The SOA is mandatory for the Principal's procurement of Security Services. The Principal reserves the right to manage the panel, including progressively rolling Suppliers off, and no reliance can be placed on stated volumes. Term is seven (7) years with three (3)×one (1) year extensions. Cap on liability of the greater of $20 million per annum or as limited by law. Subcontractors require the Principal's prior written approval. Personnel must complete a Confidentiality, Privacy and Conflict of Interest Deed.

Recommended bid strategy

Focus on demonstrating robust compliance with all mandatory requirements — particularly licensing, insurance and adherence to the Queensland Government Supplier Code of Conduct. Clearly articulate capability and capacity to deliver across diverse Queensland locations. Provide a competitive, transparent pricing structure that adheres to the SSIA base-rate and fixed-administration-fee model, emphasising value for money. Highlight innovative approaches and differentiators that align with the Queensland Procurement Policy's social and environmental objectives.
